Flyscanner reference

How each recording is analyzed

  1. The camera captures at full 1280×720 resolution. The calibrated analysis region is cropped from each frame; changing saved-video resolution does not reduce analysis resolution.
  2. Each analysis frame is converted to grayscale. A small Gaussian blur is applied only to the copy used for frame-to-frame motion comparison. Neighboring pixels are gently averaged so isolated sensor noise, compression speckles, and tiny brightness fluctuations do not look like movement. Real fly motion covers coherent groups of pixels and remains detectable. The unblurred grayscale frame is retained for brightness measurement and dark/fly classification.
  3. A dark-pixel threshold identifies likely flies. It is normally estimated from the first frame with Otsu thresholding* and constrained to a safe range, or it can be supplied manually.
  4. The intersection of the motion mask and dark-pixel mask becomes the moving-fly mask. This makes moving dark objects count more strongly than general lighting or background movement.
  5. Percentages are accumulated over the complete recording. Using percentages rather than raw pixel counts keeps results broadly comparable across analysis resolutions.

Normal scan versus 24-hour session

The 24-hour option is a session, not one 24-hour score or video. It automatically performs 144 separate ten-minute scans and groups them under one session timestamp.

This design preserves time-resolved activity: a problem or power interruption affects at most the current ten-minute part instead of risking a single enormous 24-hour file. There is intentionally no combined 24-hour activity score; use the 144 rows to examine how activity changes over time.

Activity-score calculation

score = 35 × moving_fly_area_percent_mean + 15 × motion_percent_mean

The same formula can be written as ((0.7 × moving-fly mean) + (0.3 × motion mean)) ÷ 2 × 100. Moving dark pixels supply 70% of the weighted signal, while all detected motion supplies 30%.

The result is a custom activity index—not a percentage or standardized biological unit—and it is deliberately not capped at 100. fly_area_percent_mean and motion_percent_peak provide useful diagnostics but do not directly enter the score.

Simple example

moving-fly mean = 0.2% motion mean = 0.5% score = (35 × 0.2) + (15 × 0.5) = 14.5

* Otsu thresholding: an automatic image-segmentation method that examines the grayscale brightness histogram and chooses the cutoff that best separates pixels into a darker group and a lighter group. Here, the darker group is treated as potential flies. Flyscanner constrains the automatically selected cutoff to 40–180 so unusual lighting or shadows cannot produce an extreme threshold.
